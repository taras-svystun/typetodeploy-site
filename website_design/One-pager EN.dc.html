The problem

A website built as code is fast, well-indexed and cheap to host. The trade-off is direct: its owner cannot change it.

Updating a price, a date or a photo means going back to a developer.

In practice these sites stop being updated. The information becomes wrong, and the site stops doing its job.

The solution
1

You write. A message in plain language, in a Telegram conversation.

2

The agent prepares the change. The model identifies the field concerned and prepares the exact edit to apply.

3

The change is verified. An automatic check refuses any edit outside the declared scope, before anything is written.

4

The site is updated. Committed to the repository, rebuilt, published, confirmed in the conversation.

What still needs validating

Is the customer the site owner, or the agency that maintains it? Both hypotheses are open; interviews will decide.

Does a non-technical person actually update more often? Update frequency tracked over eight weeks with a pilot group.

Is there an acceptable price for this service? Price testing with the people interviewed.

Phase 2
Step 1 Extend the scope check to the source code, not only content fields.
Step 2 Add and edit sections, pages and layout, with a preview before publishing.
Step 3 Connect the tool to an existing site that was not built for it.
